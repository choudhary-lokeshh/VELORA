# Decisions required

## Rule

Items are intentionally unresolved. No implementation, design, or provider agent may choose silently. Each accepted decision records owner, rationale, date, options, trade-offs, affected authorities, security/privacy/compliance review, migration/rollback, and ADR where architecture changes. Popularity alone is not rationale.

## Pair-lock contention behaviour

**RESOLVED — see [ADR-0019](ADR-0019-database-connection-admission.md).**

This entry previously asked whether to raise the connection pool or bound the pair-lock wait with `SET LOCAL lock_timeout`, on the premise that a waiter holding its pooled connection made the pool a hard ceiling on same-pair contention. **The premise was wrong.** Measurement showed the stall is a Bun.SQL defect — a pool that must queue for a connection while serving both transactions and autocommit queries can lose one permanently to `idle in transaction` — and it reproduces with no advisory lock present at all. Both options in the original question addressed a cause that does not exist, and both were measured to refuse every concurrent caller on a contended pair.

The decision taken is to leave the pair lock, its ordering, and its unbounded wait unchanged, and to bound in-flight database work below the pool while opening the pool at startup and never reaping it. Pool size stays at ten and admission at eight per process. A request that waits more than 250 ms for capacity gets `503` with `Retry-After: 1`; nothing about pair contention is product-visible, so no surface copy changes.

The corrected measurement is in [jobs, idempotency and concurrency](../engineering/03-jobs-idempotency-concurrency.md).
