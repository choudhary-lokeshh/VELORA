# Media storage, delivery, processing, and scanning provider eligibility

## Purpose and status

This document records what object-storage, CDN, image-processing, and malware-scanning providers currently say, in their own published policies, about a service shaped like Velora. It exists so that media provider selection is an evidence-backed decision rather than a popularity contest, and so that the reason live production media stays blocked is written down with sources rather than asserted.

It is architecture and product guidance, not legal advice, and it is not a contract. A provider's published policy is the floor, never the approval: an acceptable-use policy that fails to prohibit something is not permission for it, and several providers below reserve unilateral discretion to remove content or terminate an account. Nothing here authorises connecting a provider. That requires the full gate in [market entry gates](01-market-entry-gates.md) and [creator and content gates](03-creator-content-gates.md), a provider-specific ADR under [provider adapters](../architecture/06-provider-adapters.md), and the production gates named in [ADR-0023](../decisions/ADR-0023-media-platform-architecture.md).

Findings are dated. A policy page is a moving document; an entry whose retrieval date has aged past its review trigger is stale evidence and must be re-verified before it supports any decision. The same rule the [payment and payout provider eligibility](06-payment-provider-eligibility.md) register runs on applies here, and for the same reason.

## How Velora must describe itself to a media provider

Eligibility turns on what is actually being stored and served, and describing only the flattering half would be misrepresentation. Velora must present all of the following.

| Attribute | Value | Why a provider cares |
|---|---|---|
| Content origin | User-generated. Every byte is uploaded by a consumer or a creator, not authored by Velora | Moves the account from "our own website assets" into the category most acceptable-use policies write their removal and indemnity clauses about |
| Content type today | Still images only. Video, audio, and generic file hosting are out of scope | Narrows exposure, and rules out the providers whose terms are video-specific |
| Audience | Adults only, 18+ | Not by itself an adult-content service, but triggers closer reading at several providers |
| Distribution | Private originals; authorized derivative delivery to authenticated consumers; a small public derivative class on public creator pages | Decides whether a provider's CDN terms or its hosting terms apply, which at one provider is the whole question |
| Volume shape | Many small objects with a long tail, not a disproportionate share of video or large files | Directly addressed by one provider's CDN restriction below |
| Mature or explicit creator content | **Not enabled.** `Conditional / Compliance-Gated` in [product phases](../product/01-product-phases.md), refused by configuration, and gated by [creator and content gates](03-creator-content-gates.md) | Decides the entire eligibility picture and flips several providers from permitted to prohibited |
| Takedown capability | Trust & Safety enforcement, takedown claims, appeals, and depicted-person consent by reference already exist under [ADR-0022](../decisions/ADR-0022-trust-safety-policy-enforcement-authority.md) | Several providers require the customer to be able to act on reported content; Velora can evidence that it can |

The mature-content row has two answers and the matrix is therefore evaluated twice, exactly as the payment register is. Velora today hosts no explicit content and has no path to publishing any. Velora's recorded direction in [Creator Private Clubs](../product/03-creator-private-clubs.md) keeps mature creator content as a gated possibility. A provider approved against the first answer is not thereby approved against the second.

### Malware and content scanning

No scanning provider was assessed, because none was needed to reach a decision: the storage decision is unresolved, and a scanner is only reachable once bytes are somewhere to scan. The scanner port exists so that this stays a provider choice rather than a rebuild. `LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED` does not attach here; what attaches is the ordinary provider gate plus the question of whether scan submission constitutes a disclosure of user content to a third party under [privacy and retention](../security/03-privacy-retention.md), which must be answered before any scanner is connected.

## Delivery capability findings

These are capability facts, not eligibility facts. They are recorded because two of them constrain what the platform may honestly promise about revocation.

- **Amazon S3 presigned URLs.** "By default, all Amazon S3 objects are private, only the object owner has permission to access them." A presigned URL grants "time-limited permission"; the documented maxima are 12 hours from the console and 7 days from the CLI or SDKs. The documentation describes no mechanism for revoking an individual issued URL before it expires — "the credentials used by the presigned URL are those of the AWS user who generated the URL", so withdrawal is a credential or policy action with far wider blast radius than one asset. Source: [Sharing objects with presigned URLs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/ShareObjectPreSignedURL.html), retrieved 2026-08-16.
- **bunny.net token authentication.** "Your application signs each URL by creating a hash from your security key, the URL path, and an expiration time", with expiry expressed as a Unix timestamp in an `expires` query parameter, optional IP validation, and directory-scoped tokens in the advanced method. Source: [bunny.net token authentication](https://bunny.net/docs/cdn-token-authentication), retrieved 2026-08-16.
- **Backblaze B2 download authorization.** Not verified in this run. The application-key page documents key duration bounded at "a positive integer less than 1000 days (in seconds)" but does not cover download authorization tokens, and the page that does was not located. Source: [Backblaze application keys](https://www.backblaze.com/docs/cloud-storage-application-keys), retrieved 2026-08-16.
- **Everything else.** Private-object controls, signed delivery, and purge semantics for Google Cloud Storage, Azure, Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, and Wasabi were **not verified in this run**. A provider that has not passed the policy gate does not earn capability research, and recording an unverified capability would be the same error as recording an unverified approval.

The first finding is why [ADR-0023](../decisions/ADR-0023-media-platform-architecture.md) requires the delivery TTL to be short and requires the freeze report to state a maximum revocation exposure rather than claim instant revocation. A signed URL is a bearer credential that the issuing platform generally cannot recall.

## What this means for implementation

- **No media provider is approved.** No adapter for any provider named above exists in the repository, and none may be added by this reading. The production configuration value refuses every provider operation, and staging and production reject any other value.
- **Silence is escalated, not assumed.** For every provider whose policy is `Silent`, enabling it — even for ordinary profile images — requires written confirmation from the provider that the described product is acceptable, recorded here with its date. Velora does not build an architecture designed to keep a provider from finding out what it is serving.
- **bunny.net is the only provider that answers the mature question in writing**, and that answer is conditioned on legality and on Velora's own responsibility for what it distributes. It is a candidate, not an approval: no security review, data-residency assessment, deletion-evidence review, or operations owner exists for it.
- **Two of the assessed processing providers are already ineligible on the second answer** and restricted on the first. Any image transformation Velora relies on must therefore be something the platform can perform itself, behind a processor port, rather than a provider URL contract. That constraint is now an architecture requirement rather than a preference.
- **The Cloudflare CDN restriction is a design input, not a footnote.** An image platform served through the plain CDN on a non-Enterprise plan is the exact case that clause describes.

## Review triggers

Re-verify every entry when any of the following happens, and in any case before a provider adapter is connected:

- more than 90 days have passed since the retrieval date on a row that a decision depends on;
- a provider announces a policy change, an acquisition, or a new acceptable-use document;
- Velora's self-description above changes, including any move on the mature-content row, any addition of video or audio, or any change to what is served publicly;
- a launch country is added whose local law changes what "legal adult content" means for a provider that conditions its permission on legality.
