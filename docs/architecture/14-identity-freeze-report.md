# Identity assurance freeze report

- Freeze status: Frozen
- Freeze SHA: `f672a1a`
- Freeze date: 2026-08-20
- Architecture authority: [ADR-0024](../decisions/ADR-0024-identity-assurance-architecture.md)

## What this records

The Identity Assurance core is frozen: the domain, its invariants, and the code that carries them are complete and green, and no further work on them is planned until something below unfreezes them.

Freezing the core is not the same as being able to verify one person's age or identity. This document exists mostly to keep those apart, in the same way the [media freeze report](13-media-freeze-report.md) separates a frozen media architecture from an ability to store a byte. Velora can create a verification subject for an owner that authorized it, open one attempt under a versioned jurisdiction policy, hand a provider a stable idempotency identity, survive an ambiguous or absent answer, accept a cryptographically verified callback, append evidence that supersedes rather than overwrites, expire and revoke it, reconcile against provider truth, publish minimized standing to the owners that consume it, and show an operator privacy-minimized health — every path built and tested against a network-free deterministic adapter. It cannot verify anybody, because **no identity, age, or KYC provider is approved and no jurisdiction policy is published**, and both ports default to a value that refuses everything. Those are decisions nobody has made, not components nobody has built.

## What is frozen

The verification subject as an opaque `(owner_domain, owner_reference)` pair that no owner can read across; the attempt as a durable record of purpose, required class and threshold, pinned policy version, jurisdiction, server-selected provider, canonical input digest, caller and platform idempotency identities, and a total order that timestamps cannot tie; the lifecycle `created -> provider_starting -> provider_pending -> processing -> terminal` with six terminal outcomes and immutability after them; append-only evidence in six classes that never widen into one another, each fact carrying its normalized result, threshold context, opaque provider fact reference, validity interval, policy version, and the one fact it supersedes; one chain per subject and class, enforced at the root as well as in the middle; the provider port as a capability-declaring seam whose only two adapters refuse or are network-free; the versioned jurisdiction policy that fails closed on `UNKNOWN`, unpublished, or blocked; the read-only re-verification comparison that reports and never acts; the verified-receipt inbox that authenticates exact raw bytes before parsing and stores a digest rather than a body; leased worker processing with bounded retry and dead-lettering; bounded reconciliation that records a finding before it repairs and never starts an attempt nobody authorized; the minimized transactional outbox; the coarse readers USERS, CREATORS, PAYOUTS, and TRUST & SAFETY consume; the read-only Admin aggregate and exact-reference projection behind a one-time ADR-0017 authorization; and the indexes that keep every one of those reads proportional to one subject rather than to everything ever recorded.

Eight migrations carry it, `0046_identity-assurance-foundation` through `0053_identity-evidence-root`. Six tables are owned by IDENTITY, all prefixed `identity_`: subjects, attempts, evidence, provider events, reconciliation findings, and the outbox. No other domain writes one and no other domain reads one; a test asserts that no `identity_` table holds a foreign key to a table outside the domain, and every owner reaches evidence through a published reader rather than through the schema. That last part is carried by the readers being the only exported way in, and is not asserted by a check that would fail if somebody imported the schema directly — a gap worth naming rather than implying.

IDENTITY publishes exactly three HTTP routes, and the absence of a fourth is a decision rather than an omission: `POST /v1/identity/provider-events` for verified callbacks, and two read-only Admin routes. There is no consumer or creator route that starts a verification, because V1 deliberately contains the platform core and not a verification product, and a start route with no approved provider behind it could only refuse.

## What the pipeline repair was

Recorded because a freeze rests on hosted evidence, and for a while that evidence was not being produced. The hosted verification job began exceeding its one-hour limit and was cancelled mid-gate at `77957a0`. The canonical `pnpm ci:verify` graph had not regressed at all — it ran 14m33s, 15m07s, and 15m05s across the same runs. What grew was `playwright install --with-deps`, which fetches 181 Ubuntu packages and 114 MB from a distribution mirror on every run; that mirror degraded to kilobytes per second, and one 7 MB font package alone took 21 minutes. The first repair raised the job budget from 60 to 90 minutes, which moved the ceiling without bounding anything. The second cached those packages by pinned browser version, gave provisioning its own 25-minute limit so a cold cache against a degraded mirror fails by itself, and returned the whole-job budget to 60 minutes. Provisioning now takes 39 to 55 seconds against the 60 minutes it had reached, and no verification stage was skipped, shortened, or made optional to achieve it.

## What is not frozen, and why

**LIVE IDENTITY AND AGE VERIFICATION IS BLOCKED.** Not by an incomplete implementation but by ports with no approved value behind them and decisions nobody has made:

- **No approved provider.** `IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_PROVIDER` defaults to `unavailable`, which refuses every provider operation, and staging and production refuse to start with any other value. Primary-source research recorded in [provider eligibility](../compliance/09-identity-verification-provider-eligibility.md), dated 2026-08-18, approves nobody: Persona, Veriff, Entrust/Onfido, Sumsub, Stripe Identity, Yoti, Jumio, and Incode each carry unresolved written-confirmation gaps. Two carry specific technical or eligibility findings rather than merely absent paperwork — Jumio's public V3 callback contract documents IP allowlisting rather than a raw-body signature, which does not meet the callback authenticity requirement, and Stripe Identity's restricted-business and use-case rules overlap what Velora is. Silence about Velora's business is treated as unapproved rather than as permission.
- **No published jurisdiction policy.** `IDENTITY_JURISDICTION_POLICY` defaults to `unpublished`, which answers `UNKNOWN` for every country, and `UNKNOWN` refuses the verification path. Which jurisdictions require which assurance tier, which methods qualify, what the validity and re-verification windows are, and what an accessible alternative path must be are all `LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED`.
- **No retention, residency, or deletion schedule.** How long attempts, evidence, verified receipts, reconciliation findings, and provider facts are kept, and what a deletion or correction request does to each, is `LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED`. No duration is invented in code, reconciliation deliberately performs no deletion, export, correction, or provider erasure, and no correctness rule depends on a row being gone — so an approved schedule later applies as a deletion pass rather than as a redesign.
- **No manual review, override, or revocation authority.** No human can grant, refuse, or revoke assurance through any route. The mechanism for a successor fact exists and the authority does not, and an operator revoking evidence with no approved evidence standard, separation of duties, expiry, or appeal behind it would be an unaudited action on somebody's identity record.
- **No admin sign-in implementation.** Every Platform Admin route requires the Platform Admin audience and a fresh phishing-resistant assurance, and no approved verifier produces one. The Identity operator surface is unreachable in every environment, and the exact-subject read additionally consumes a one-time ADR-0017 authorization bound to both the subject and the executing session.
- **No alert routing or on-call ownership.** The aggregate projection publishes backlog, expiry, and reconciliation health, and nothing routes any of it to anybody.

**LIVE KYC AND PAYOUT VERIFICATION IS BLOCKED**, separately and on more gates than the above. Commercial-KYC evidence has a class, a contract, and a coarse reader, and that is deliberately all it has: evidence is not payout permission. Beyond every identity gate, payouts additionally need `PAYOUTS_PROVIDER`, `PAYOUTS_POLICY`, `BILLING_TAX_AUTHORITY`, and `BILLING_COMMERCE_ELIGIBILITY`, each of which defaults to a refusing value that staging and production reject, and creator payouts remain a later product phase. **No `kycReady`, `commercialEligible`, or `payoutAllowed` boolean exists anywhere**, because a single flag is exactly how commercial KYC quietly becomes payout authorization.

Consumer stronger-assurance and Creator verification workflows are Phase 2, and commercial-KYC and payout-readiness product exposure is Phase 3, both by [ADR-0024](../decisions/ADR-0024-identity-assurance-architecture.md) and [product phases](../product/01-product-phases.md). They are not built, and that is scope rather than omission: the V1 core exists so that moving verified evidence behind one isolated fail-closed authority is platform risk reduction rather than a consumer feature.

## The invariants, and where each is enforced

**A provider's answer is evidence about a person, never permission for one.** Every invariant below is a consequence of that sentence.

Identity never authenticates anybody. It issues no session, assigns no role, and grants no product access; AUTH remains the sole authentication authority and IDENTITY links to a principal by opaque reference only. An owner re-authorizes every request and re-evaluates current evidence under its own policy, so a provider success cannot become an authorization by arriving.

No client chooses anything that matters. Provider, jurisdiction policy, required evidence class, and threshold are selected at the composition and policy boundary from validated server configuration; no route, header, query value, client field, or caller domain reaches either registry. The two adapters are `unavailable`, which refuses, and `local-test`, which is deterministic and network-free and which staging and production refuse to start with.

Nothing that identifies a person is stored. No raw document, document number, name, address, exact date of birth, selfie, video, biometric template, tax identifier, bank detail, callback body, or reusable hosted link has a column, and a test enumerates every column of every `identity_` table and asserts that none of them is one. A hosted link is a secret returned once to the authorized actor and never persisted, logged, published, or placed in the outbox.

Evidence is append-only and derived rather than declared. Grant, refusal, expiry, and revocation are separate immutable facts; a current answer is the non-stale tip of one supersession chain evaluated against current policy. The database refuses a second successor of one fact and a second root of one subject and class, so a subject can never hold two current tips of one class. A later attempt outranks an earlier fact whatever timestamp a provider stamped on it, which is what stops a delayed success from resurrecting assurance a revocation already took away.

Classes never widen into one another. A verified identity is not adult assurance; creator identity is not commercial KYC; depicted-person identity is not depicted-person adult threshold; and the published readers resolve by owner domain, exact owner reference, purpose, and class, so none of them can answer for another. `self_declared` stays USERS truth and is never verified evidence — a person who only declared their own age has no Identity standing at all, and the reader says so rather than returning an absence an owner could misread.

Provider I/O never holds a transaction. The attempt and its stable provider-idempotency identity are committed first, the adapter is called outside any transaction, and the result is bound in a second transaction — so a timeout after the commit is recoverable by lookup under the same key rather than ambiguous. A test asserts no PostgreSQL transaction is open around the adapter call.

Callbacks are authenticated before they are understood. The endpoint verifies provider, account, environment, and a signature over the exact raw bytes before parsing, bounds the body size before that, and answers an authentication failure opaquely; only verified receipts enter the inbox, unique by provider, account, environment, and event identity, and the body is discarded once a digest and a normalized allow-list are recorded. Duplicates are acknowledged identically to the first receipt.

Every duty is durable and every effect idempotent. PostgreSQL holds the claim as a lease rather than memory, BullMQ is a wake-up rather than a record, retry and dead-lettering are bounded, and reconciliation is the second net rather than the only one. Reconciliation records a normalized finding before it repairs, applies a missing provider state through the same append-only path a verified callback uses, records a `created` attempt as stuck rather than starting it, and treats a retrieval failure as a redacted operational failure rather than as an answer.

Reading is scoped and uniform. A cross-subject, cross-Creator, or cross-participant read is answered exactly as an absent record; a depicted-person read answers only for the exact participant and subject pairing; an owner reference never resolves across owner domains; and the Admin surface has an aggregate read and an exact-reference read and no list, search, export, mutation, grant, refusal, or revocation.

## What the hardening phases found

Recorded because the value of an audit is in what it caught:

- **The evidence chain was enforced in its middle but not at its root.** `identity_evidence_supersedes_uk` forbade two successors of one fact, so no chain could branch after it started, and nothing forbade a second root. Every root is a non-superseded tip, and the current-evidence read takes one row without an order, so a subject holding two tips of one class had no single current answer and a revoked tip could be passed over for a granted one. `0053_identity-evidence-root` makes one chain per subject and class a database truth, and a concurrent second first-fact now loses on a constraint rather than forking the chain. No behaviour test could have reached it: every test that creates evidence creates it through the path that links to the current tip, so the fork is only constructible by a hostile read of what the constraints do not say.
- **The Admin history read sorted a subject's whole retained history to return one page.** `identity_attempts_subject_history_idx` led with `(subject_id, purpose, sequence)`, and the exact-reference read pages one subject's attempts across every purpose in lifecycle order, so the index could not serve that order at all. Measured on the real schema at two hundred thousand subjects, with the read's own subject carrying a five-hundred-attempt mixed-purpose history, by putting the old index back and watching the planner: the replaced index produced an index scan of all 501 retained attempts followed by a `top-N heapsort` to return 51 rows, touching 52 buffers, while the narrowed index answers the same query with a backward index scan of exactly 51 rows and 6 buffers and no sort at all. The cost of the old plan is proportional to everything the subject has ever accumulated rather than to the page asked for, which is the shape that becomes an outage rather than a slow screen. `0052_identity-operations-scale` narrows the index to `(subject_id, sequence)`; `(subject_id, purpose)` lookups keep their coverage from the idempotency and active-attempt uniques, so nothing lost an access path.
- **The Redis restart test proved the server was up and not that it was reachable.** Found by the stability sequence rather than by a review: on the sixth run it failed with "publishes no host port for 6379" against a container that was already healthy. The test restarts Redis, waits for `redis-cli ping` to answer `PONG`, and then resolves the new ephemeral host port — but `docker exec` runs inside the container and never crosses the port binding, so `PONG` says nothing about whether Docker has republished it. On a loaded machine that gap is real and the read happened inside it. The mapping is now an eventual condition, the same way the outage on the other side of the restart already was. Nothing about the assertion changed: the suite still proves the queue recovers, the job survives, and the key is still in Redis.

- **The AUTH rate-limit test asserted a guarantee a fixed window does not make.** Found by the fifteenth run of the stability sequence, which sent twenty-five authentication attempts against a limit of twenty and saw not one refusal. The counter window is fixed — `floor(now / windowSeconds)` — so a burst straddling a boundary is split across two windows and each side can stay under the limit. That is the accepted trade-off of a counter this cheap, and the limiter was behaving exactly as designed; the test was asserting something stronger than the design promises, and got away with it because a twenty-five request loop takes 190 ms and a minute boundary is rarely inside it. The clock is now injected the way every other time-dependent component here already injects one — the harness could already freeze the auth runtime's clock, and the limiter was silently ignoring it — so the test holds the window still and asserts where the ceiling actually falls: the first twenty attempts are not refused and every attempt after them is. That is a stronger assertion than the one that flaked, not a weaker one.

- **A re-verification in flight withholds the standing a person currently holds.** This is the safe direction and is exactly why a stale success cannot resurrect assurance, so it was recorded rather than changed. It is also not a finished answer: a Phase 2 workflow that opens an attempt without deciding what the person may do while it is open would strand somebody who is verified today. It is written against the open identity policy decision in [DECISIONS_REQUIRED](../decisions/DECISIONS_REQUIRED.md) rather than resolved here.

## The evidence this rests on

Twenty consecutive runs of the API integration suite, on the frozen tree, with no retries and no reruns: **1,069 tests across 61 files, 1,069 passing and none failing in every one of the twenty.** Every run was a complete suite against commit `f672a1a` with a clean worktree verified before the first and after every one of them, and the runs took between 206 and 246 seconds. The sequence was the whole proof — a suite that passes on a second attempt has been shown to be flaky rather than stable, so any failure voided it and restarted it from the first run after a root-cause fix. That happened twice, and both times the fix was to the thing that was actually wrong rather than to the assertion that caught it.

A first attempt at the sequence reached nine consecutive clean runs — 1,069 passing and none failing in each — before the process supervising it was stopped from outside during the tenth. No test failed and nothing about the tree changed. It was restarted from run one rather than continued from run ten, because twenty consecutive runs means twenty, and nine plus eleven with an interruption between them is a different and weaker claim.

One host intervention happened during the sequence and is recorded rather than left implicit. Each integration run leaves an anonymous Docker volume behind, and by the sixth run 256 of them held 26.4 GB, with run duration climbing from 272 to 503 seconds as the container host's disk filled. Unreferenced anonymous volumes were pruned between runs; the named `velora-local_postgres-data` volume was preserved, no repository file changed, the tree stayed clean and on the same commit, and no run was re-run or discarded. It is disk housekeeping on the machine underneath the proof rather than a change to what the proof is about.

Inside every one of those twenty, the Identity suites carry the domain: foundation, orchestration, provider events, the USERS and SAFETY cutovers, reconciliation, operations scale, and the adversarial [`identity-red-team`](../../apps/api/test/integration/identity-red-team.test.ts) suite whose attacks the [threat model](../security/11-identity-verification-threat-model.md) records. The cutover suites run the real migrations against real PostgreSQL and assert both the moved history and the rollback when an assertion fails, so the two ownership transfers are proved by execution rather than by inspection.

Five consecutive runs each of the surface suites on the same tree, every run passing and every task force-executed rather than served from the build cache — verified by counting twenty cache bypasses and zero cache hits in each run, and by the five result sets being byte-identical: the API unit suite at 265 across 20 files, Consumer Web at 51, Consumer Mobile at 32, Creator Studio at 38, Platform Admin at 10, the consumer and creator API clients at 10 each, the generated API client at 11, validation at 18, config at 54, design tokens at 2, and the browser end-to-end suite at 65 passing across three engines with 19 skipped for the same pre-existing structural reason the media freeze recorded — a browser that requires HTTPS before it will keep the session cookie cannot run a signed-in journey against a plain-HTTP local server, and the skip names that condition rather than the test.

Beneath all of it, a fresh canonical `pnpm ci:verify` over the frozen tree: toolchain and frozen-install verification, workspace policy, formatting, lint, domain boundaries, typecheck, contract generation checked against the committed OpenAPI document and generated client, the authorization policy check, unit tests, the integration suite against real PostgreSQL and Redis, an uncached build of every app, browser end-to-end tests, the mobile doctor, compose validation, secret scanning, whitespace and hygiene across 753 tracked and untracked files, and the dependency security gate. Exit 0, and green on the hosted pipeline for every commit in this milestone at its exact SHA, one of them on a second attempt after a provisioning timeout cancelled the first.

No test was weakened, skipped, or retried to reach any of this, and no identity, privacy, or safety control was relaxed to make one pass. Where a gate went red it was fixed and the whole gate re-run from the beginning rather than the failing step re-run on its own: a Prettier disagreement with generated migration metadata, four `restrict-template-expressions` errors in a scale suite that had never been gated, and a missing final newline in a generated migration.

## Explicit privacy and safety answers

Asked and answered directly, because a freeze report that leaves these to inference is not a freeze report:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Raw passport stored | **NO** |
| Raw national ID stored | **NO** |
| Aadhaar stored | **NO** |
| Raw selfie stored | **NO** |
| Biometric template stored | **NO** |
| Exact date of birth stored | **NO** |
| Client-authoritative verification | **NO** |
| Public legal identity exposure | **NO** |
| Test provider activatable in production | **NO** |

No column of any `identity_` table holds a document, document number, image, video, biometric template, name, address, or date of birth, and a test enumerates every column and asserts it. Age is carried as a threshold outcome — an opaque `adult_threshold` class with a `threshold_context` such as `adult-18-plus` — rather than as a birth date, because a platform that needs to know somebody is over eighteen does not need to know when they were born, and storing the date would create a lifelong identifier to answer a boolean question. No approved authority for an exact date exists, and none is invented.

Verification is server-authoritative end to end: the caller supplies an owner-authorized subject and purpose and nothing else that decides anything, a redirect return is a read hint that grants no evidence, and only a cryptographically verified callback or a provider retrieval produces a fact. Legal identity is never public — evidence is opaque to every owner, the published readers carry a coarse standing and no provider or evidence handle, and the outbox carries opaque references, class, lifecycle, policy version, validity, and correlation and nothing else. The `local-test` provider and policy are rejected at startup in staging and production by a configuration refusal that runs before the application exists, so no environment variable, header, request, or feature flag can activate a fake verifier where it would matter.

## Status

**IDENTITY ASSURANCE + CREATOR VERIFICATION CORE: FROZEN**

**LIVE IDENTITY / AGE VERIFICATION: BLOCKED** — no approved identity or age provider; no published jurisdiction policy, so every country answers `UNKNOWN` and refuses; no approved assurance methods or thresholds; no approved retention, residency, or deletion schedule; no approved biometric legal basis, notices, or consent wording; no accessible alternative or manual route; no appeal or correction workflow; no admin sign-in implementation, which leaves the operator surface unreachable in every environment; and no alert routing or on-call ownership.

**LIVE KYC / PAYOUT VERIFICATION: BLOCKED** — separately, on every gate above plus: no approved KYC scope, sanctions or PEP requirements; no payout provider; no published payout policy; no tax authority; no commerce eligibility; and creator payouts remain a later product phase. Commercial-KYC evidence is evidence, and no boolean anywhere turns it into payout permission.

**MATURE CONTENT PRODUCTION ENABLEMENT: BLOCKED** — separately, on its own gates, and unchanged by this milestone. The [trust and safety freeze report](12-trust-safety-freeze-report.md) is the authority for that block, and the depicted-person evidence classes this milestone added do not weaken it: they give the mature-content gate a fail-closed answer to consult rather than a missing one.

## What unfreezes this

Any of: an identity, age, or KYC provider becomes eligible under a written confirmation naming Velora's business model and is selected in a provider ADR; a jurisdiction policy is legally approved and published for at least one country and purpose; retention, residency, and deletion schedules are approved; a biometric legal basis, notices, and consent wording are approved; an accessible alternative or manual review route is approved with its evidence standard, separation of duties, expiry, and appeal; an admin sign-in implementation is approved; or alert routing and on-call ownership are assigned. None of those is a code change first — the adapter boundary is where a provider lands, and the policy ports are where approved terms land.

The review triggers on [provider eligibility](../compliance/09-identity-verification-provider-eligibility.md) apply: its findings are dated 2026-08-18, and an entry whose retrieval date has aged past its trigger is stale evidence that must be re-verified before it supports any decision.
